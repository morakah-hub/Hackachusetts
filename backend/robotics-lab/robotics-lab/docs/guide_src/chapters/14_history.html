Project development history

The project's evolution reconstructed from the Git history (18 commits, 14–19 October 2026), the commit messages, the source at each milestone and the research reports. Where documentation is thin (v0.1, v0.2) only what the evidence supports is stated.

The shape of the history

Verified from Git

The branch master still points to the v0.1 commit (26f46f9). Each later milestone was developed on its own branch, started from the previous milestone, and was never merged back: feature/mpc-v0.2 → feature/mpc-v0.3-robustness → feature/mpc-v0.4-reliability → feature/v0.5-motion-planning → feature/v0.5.1-robustness-demo → feature/v0.5.2-clearance-fix. The newest branch therefore contains the whole history. Merging into master requires an explicit decision by the owner (CLAUDE.md: "Do not commit or push without the user's explicit approval").

Initialization (184bfd6, 14 Oct)

A scaffold of 24 files, all empty except .gitignore and requirements.txt: the module layout (controllers/, tasks/, simulation/, robots/, environments/) was decided before any code was written, including controllers/mpc.py, tasks/reach.py and tasks/pick_place.py as placeholders. The first two were later filled; pick_place.py is still empty.

v0.1 — first working Panda simulation (26f46f9, 14 Oct)

  • Implemented. The Menagerie Panda vendored unmodified; scenes table, empty, obstacles; the engine (MjSpec attachment at robot_mount); the runner with viewer and headless modes; JointTargetTask with minimum-jerk moves through three waypoints in a loop; the JointPIDController outer loop on the position servos.
  • Problem solved. A physically simulated arm moving under closed-loop control, with motion only through actuators.
  • Experiments and tests. 16 tests (all still present in tests/test_simulation.py), e.g. the arm falls under gravity without actuation, the integral removes gravity sag, the closed loop reaches all waypoints with small error.
  • Not documented. v0.1 has no research report; its performance figures appear only in CLAUDE.md (PID max tracking error < 4 mrad, steady state ~0.1 mrad), which the later benchmark confirms (3.42 mrad on demo).

v0.2 — experimental joint-space MPC on the servos (71d008c, 17 Oct)

  • Implemented. JointMPCController optimizing servo setpoints with OSQP; MuJoCoPredictionModel (linearization from mjd_transitionFD); PID fallback; controller selection by config/CLI; the first benchmark (benchmark.py, configs/benchmark.yaml).
  • Results (README "Benchmark results (v0.2)"). PID tracked moving references more tightly (demo 3.4 vs 7.8 mrad); the MPC saturated actuators on 21% of physics steps in demo because each setpoint step causes a ~1 ms clipped torque spike; in the wrist scenario 275 of 450 updates fell back to PID. MPC was better at rest, against the push (2.0 vs 3.4 mrad), and respected speed limits PID ignored. The commit message itself labels the controller "experimental".
  • Tests. 46 (per the v0.3 report).

v0.3 — robustness, torque-level control, Cartesian reaching (646955d…b3bf48b, 17 Oct)

  • Audit and the one MPC bug. Tools verified the QP objective term by term (6e-14). The audit found that after a fallback the servo MPC anchored to its stale solution instead of the command actually applied; fixing it reduced wrist fallbacks from 275 to 43 of 450 and overruns from 236 to 31 (later 1).
  • Torque actuation. Panda(actuation="torque") in code via MjSpec; ComputedTorqueController; TorqueMPCController with normalized torques (fixing a 10-orders-of-magnitude conditioning problem) and an exact-penalty weight raised from 1e5 to 1e6 after a 2.5 rad step was planned at 3.8× v_max.
  • Cartesian reaching. ArmKinematics (DLS IK), CartesianReachTask, IK plus straight-path collision checking (targets are rejected, not avoided).
  • Benchmark. Torque MPC 2.8 µrad vs CTC 63 µrad on demo; under a 30 N push, torque controllers deflected 12–35 mrad (no integral action); with 15% mass error MPC degraded to 1.9 mrad; on fast/wrist the torque MPC had 169/532 mrad error (explained in v0.4). Speed-ups: forward-difference Jacobians and fixed CSC slots.
  • Tests. 85. Report: docs/research/v0.3-results.md.

v0.4 — reliability, feasibility, disturbance rejection (11608c6…b807baa, 18 Oct)

  • Root cause of fast/wrist. The references were infeasible (1.43× the joint-7 speed limit); a lower bound showed no speed-limited controller can beat 147/304 mrad. Classified as "not a controller bug".
  • Trajectory generation. tasks/trajectory.py: time-scaled quintic and double-S, torque feasibility, TrajectoryInfeasible; new *_feasible scenarios while keeping the historical ones.
  • Constraints. Separate reporting of planned versus actual violations; a 0.5% velocity margin removed all 369/578 tiny over-speed steps of the torque MPC. The servo MPC's intersample violations could not be fixed this way.
  • Disturbance observer. Offset-free torque MPC with 500 Hz feedthrough; push error 12.1 → 3.6 mrad (MPC), 35.2 → 3.1 mrad (CTC + observer); mass errors ±25% handled without instability. Integral action for CTC evaluated and rejected as the main remedy.
  • Orientation metrics, sensor-noise option, timing profile (50 Hz fits, 100 Hz does not).
  • Not fixed. The servo MPC still saturates the wrist servos (135 mrad on feasible wrist motion vs 3.4 for PID).
  • Tests. 122. Report: v0.4-results.md.

v0.5 — collision-aware motion planning (e117058…ce3e907, 19 Oct)

  • Implemented. planning/: CollisionChecker with 20 mm clearance via margins and lever-arm edge sampling; RRTConnect; shortcutting; multiple IK postures; MotionPlanner; PlannedJointTask; scenes planning_demo and planning_narrow; the planning benchmark.
  • Three defects found by measurement. MuJoCo's midphase dropped ~20% of near misses inside a margin (disabled in the checker); two shortcutting bugs made "optimized" paths longer; IK posture selection, not RRT, caused 8/20 failures in the narrow passage (fixed with local IK seeds).
  • Results. Wall, obstacle-block and narrow-passage scenes planned 20/20; all 24 planned executions succeeded with 0 contacts; all 8 unchecked direct executions collided.
  • Architecture. Planning happens once at task construction; controllers unchanged.
  • Tests. 162. Report: v0.5-results.md.

v0.5.1 — demo video and wall-geometry replanning (8309559, 2197959, 19 Oct)

  • Implemented. environment_overrides (change obstacle geometry in code per episode); the six-variant × five-seed experiment (robustness_experiment.py); ExactClearance; the offscreen video renderer.
  • Results. Replanning 30/30; executions 60/60 with 0 contacts; old plans invalid in 20/20 episodes where the change mattered (they would have penetrated the new wall by 3–67 mm).
  • Finding. MuJoCo's box-box margin contacts are unreliable, so the v0.5 "15 mm" certificate did not hold for fingertip pads against box obstacles. Documented and pinned with a strict expected-failure test; not fixed because a planner change needed approval.
  • Tests. 176 (one expected failure). Report: v0.5.1-demo-brief.md.

v0.5.2 — collision-checker correctness (5dbeffd, 19 Oct)

  • Implemented. An audit tool with certified ground truth; exact box-box distances in the checker (exact_box_box) with a provably safe pre-filter; vectorized contact reading; exact clearance metrics in the benchmarks; a profiling tool; video renderer labelled v0.5.2 that refuses to overwrite earlier videos.
  • Results. Only box-box margin contacts were wrong among 11 pair types; with the old checker 3 of 113 benchmark plans came within 18.5–19.99 mm, now 0; success rates and execution accuracy unchanged; planning 6–46% slower.
  • Tests. 189, none expected to fail; the v0.5.1 expected failure was converted to a checker test plus a pinned MuJoCo-behaviour test. Report: v0.5.2-results.md.

Patterns across the history

  • Each milestone first audited the previous one, and several "failures" turned out to be measurement or specification problems (infeasible references, misreported distances) rather than algorithm bugs.
  • Historical benchmark scenarios and controller variants (torque_mpc_v03, torque_ctc) were kept unchanged as regression baselines; new scenarios were added beside them.
  • Two MuJoCo behaviours that undermined collision claims (midphase and box-box margins) were found by checking results against an independent measurement.
  • The servo MPC was never made competitive with PID; it remains in the code as a documented negative result.
