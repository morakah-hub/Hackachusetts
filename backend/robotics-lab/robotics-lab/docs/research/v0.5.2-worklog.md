# v0.5.2 worklog: collision-checker correctness (box-box clearance)

Branch `feature/v0.5.2-clearance-fix`, from v0.5.1 `2197959`. Scope: the box-box near-miss
problem found in v0.5.1 (brief §4.4) and nothing else. No new planning method, no grasping.
Results and conclusions: [`v0.5.2-results.md`](v0.5.2-results.md).

## 1. Which pairs are affected

Collidable geoms in the four scenes (`planning_demo`, `planning_narrow`, `obstacles`, `table`):

- robot: 12 mesh hulls (link0–link7, hand, two finger bodies) and 10 boxes, all in the two
  finger bodies: one 17 × 8 × 17 mm fingertip pad and four 6 × 4 × 6-7 mm "teeth" per finger;
- environment: boxes (table top, four legs, wall, ceiling, obstacle_1), one cylinder
  (obstacle_2), the floor plane.

MuJoCo routes these pairs to different narrow-phase functions: mesh-anything and box-cylinder
go through the convex (GJK/EPA) path, box-plane and mesh-plane through the plane routines, and
box-box through MuJoCo's dedicated box-box routine. Only the last one was suspect.

## 2. Is mj_geomDistance an acceptable replacement? First probe

Scratch probe: the v0.5.1 two-box model, 3000 random poses, compared with an OSQP QP for the
distance between the two boxes. `mj_geomDistance` never over-estimated, but under-estimated by
up to 1.9 mm in 3 of 1127 separated poses. Its witness points lay inside both boxes and a
brute-force vertex/edge computation agreed with it, so the QP had not converged, not MuJoCo.
Lesson: a QP is a poor ground truth near degenerate (parallel-face) configurations. The audit
therefore uses certificates instead (§3). The probe also showed that `mj_geomDistance` ignores
`geom_margin` (identical results with margin 0 and 20 mm) and does not depend on `distmax`.

## 3. The audit tool (`tools/audit_clearance.py`)

One isolated two-geom model per pair type, with the real Panda geometry (mesh vertices copied
from the compiled model) and the checker's solver flags (midphase off, same CCD settings). The
environment geom gets margin 20 mm, as in the checker. Random poses within 40 mm of the
obstacle. Truth that does not trust MuJoCo:

- overlap: LP feasibility (scipy `linprog`, HiGHS): some convex combination of the robot geom's
  vertices inside the obstacle. Cylinders: inscribed and circumscribed 64-gon prisms; poses in
  between (within ~3 µm of the surface) are "ambiguous" and not counted;
- separation: the witness points returned by `mj_geomDistance` must lie in their geoms (upper
  bound), and the support function of both geoms along the witness normal gives a lower bound
  (separating plane). Certified when the gap is < 1 µm;
- "nothing within distmax": a separating plane along the direction from the robot geom's vertex
  centroid to the nearest obstacle point must prove the pose is > 40 mm away; otherwise the pose
  counts as uncertified (first quick run: 5 such poses, all > 200 mm away once handled);
- plane: exact (lowest vertex).

Full run: 11 pair types × 4000 counted poses. Results in the report, §2.

## 4. Checker design

Options considered:

1. Replace `mj_collision` by `mj_geomDistance` for every robot-environment pair. Correct, but
   ~150-180 pairs per configuration from Python: ~250-480 µs per check vs ~25 µs.
2. Change the fingertip boxes to box-shaped meshes in the checker's model copy (then MuJoCo's
   GJK path would handle them). Needs recompiling from the spec; the checker is built from a
   compiled `MjModel` and should see exactly the plant's geometry. Rejected.
3. **Keep `mj_collision` for everything except box-box robot/obstacle pairs, ignore MuJoCo's
   contacts for those, and call `mj_geomDistance` for the ones a conservative bound cannot rule
   out.** Chosen. The audit supports keeping MuJoCo's margin contacts for every other type.

The pre-filter must never drop a pair closer than the clearance. First version: point-to-OBB
distance of the robot box's center minus its bounding radius (valid lower bound). It cost
~23 µs per configuration, more than `mj_collision`, because it is ~14 tiny numpy calls at
~1.5 µs each. Reworked:

- contacts read as arrays (`data.contact.geom1/geom2/dist`) instead of iterating `MjContact`
  structs in Python (also speeds up the v0.5 path);
- obstacle frames cached when the obstacle bodies are welded to the world;
- the test reduced to "robot box center inside the obstacle's world AABB grown by
  clearance + bounding radius" (an AABB contains the box, so a center outside it on one axis is
  provably at least that far from the box), with the pair mask folded into the threshold.

Remaining cost: up to ~25 µs per configuration on the planner workload (§6.1 of the report). Further options (inflating the finger meshes'
margins so that MuJoCo's own broadphase/narrowphase flags fingers near an obstacle) would
borrow MuJoCo's C broadphase but complicate the argument and self-collision semantics; not done.

`exact_box_box=False` restores the v0.5 behavior bit for bit (no exact pairs, nothing skipped),
for comparisons only. The independent re-validation in the planning benchmark always uses the
corrected checker.

## 5. Test configurations

The fingertip-wall and fingertip-table configurations in `tests/test_clearance.py` were found by
random jitter (σ = 0.06 rad) around wall plans and IK poses above the table, classified with
`mj_geomDistance` per pair. The near-miss configuration was refined by local descent (minimize
the pad-wall distance subject to: v0.5 accepts it, every non-box pair ≥ 20.5 mm). Rounding
configurations to 4 decimals moved a finger mesh from 20.0 to 19.98 mm in one case, so the
near-miss configuration is stored at full precision and every test re-measures what it relies on.

Robot-level exposure of v0.5: random search plus local descent found v0.5 accepting a pad
17.8 mm from the wall (2.2 mm inside the clearance) and nothing below 20 mm at the table. In
isolation the same pad geometry is missed at 1.85-2.1 mm. The difference: on the robot, the
finger mesh, which MuJoCo checks correctly, sits right behind the pad and usually triggers
first. The isolated cases are pinned in tests with the real pad size (pad-table 2.13 mm,
pad-wall 2.30 mm) and the v0.5.1 pose (1.25 mm).

## 6. The v0.5.1 expected failure

`test_box_box_margin_contact_is_generated_inside_the_margin` asserted that raw MuJoCo generates
a contact; that remains false in MuJoCo 3.16, whatever the checker does. It was split into:

- `test_checker_rejects_box_box_near_miss_inside_the_margin`: the same pose through the
  corrected checker, which rejects it ("within clearance (1.2 mm < 20 mm)"), and through the
  v0.5 mode, which accepts it;
- `test_mujoco_box_box_still_misses_this_margin_contact`: pins the MuJoCo behavior (no xfail).
  If it fails after an upgrade, MuJoCo changed; re-run the audit before relying on contacts.
