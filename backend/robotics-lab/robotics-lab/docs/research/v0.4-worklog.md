## Stage 2: why torque MPC loses to CTC on fast/wrist (2026-10-18)

Tools: `tools/diagnose_torque_mpc.py` (instrumented runs), reference check with
`tasks/trajectory.check_reference`. Results saved to `results/v0.4_diagnostics/`.

1. The references are infeasible. Min-jerk peak speed = 1.875 |dq| / T. `fast` asks joint 7
   for 3.72 rad/s (1.43x its 2.61 rad/s limit) for 350 physics steps; `wrist` asks 3.75 rad/s
   (1.44x) for 422 steps. Accelerations are within the datasheet limits (0.57x / 0.29x).
   Any controller that respects the speed limit must lag: the lag accumulated during one
   excess interval is L = integral (|qd_ref| - v_max)+ dt = 294 mrad (fast), 608 mrad (wrist).
   With unlimited preview a controller can lead by L/2 before and lag L/2 after, so the
   lowest achievable max error is ~147 mrad (fast) and ~304 mrad (wrist).
2. Torque MPC respects the limit (pred 1.0000, actual 1.0006-1.0007) and gets 169 mrad (fast,
   within 15% of the bound) and 532 mrad (wrist). CTC ignores the limit (1.43x) and tracks.
3. Remove only the velocity limit: torque MPC tracks fast to 0.1 mrad max and wrist to
   < 0.05 mrad (better than CTC's 0.54 / 0.29 mrad). Same poses with feasible timing
   (fast 1.5 s, wrist 3.0 s moves; 0.95x / 0.96x peak speed): < 0.05 mrad. So the MPC math,
   linearization and solver are not the cause.
4. Horizon (preview length) is the only setting that changes the wrist result: N=10 (0.2 s)
   594 mrad, N=25 (0.5 s) 532, N=40 (0.8 s) 439; 100 Hz N=25 (0.25 s) 586, 100 Hz N=50
   (0.5 s) 535. Longer preview lets the MPC lead the reference before the infeasible part,
   moving it towards the 304 mrad bound.
5. No effect (identical to 3 digits): solver eps 1e-7 (vs 1e-5), warm start off, hard instead
   of soft state constraints, position weight 1e4/1e6, velocity weight 0. No solver failures,
   no inaccurate solutions, no fallbacks, max slack <= 1e-5. No torque saturation
   (first planned torque never at its limit): actuator saturation is not involved.
6. Prediction accuracy: one-interval error of x[1] vs the next measurement is <= 7e-6 rad and
   <= 9.3e-4 rad/s. The LTI model's *open-loop* error against a nonlinear rollout of the same
   planned torques grows ~k^3 along the horizon: mean max-joint error 2.5e-7 rad at k = 1,
   1.4e-4 at k = 5, 1.9e-3 at k = 10, 2.4-4.8e-2 at k = 25 (0.5 s), worst on joints 4 and 6.
   It does not hurt tracking (receding horizon re-linearizes every 20 ms; perfect tracking
   in item 3 has the same LTI error) but it means predictions late in the horizon, and
   constraint predictions there, are approximate.
7. Actual velocity violations: the soft constraint is active at exactly v_max over the
   infeasible stretch, so the plan rides the bound; the real arm then exceeds it by the
   prediction error (max 1.7e-3 rad/s, 0.07%) on 369 / 578 physics steps. These are
   prediction-mismatch violations of a constraint held at its bound, not slack usage
   (slack <= 3e-6). Addressed in Stage 4.

Root-cause classification: primarily C (infeasible reference) with the controller correctly
enforcing the speed limit that CTC ignores; B only in the sense that the 0.5 s preview limits
how early the MPC can lead; D explains the small residual violations. Not A (no math bug
found; tracking is exact when the constraint is removed), not E.

## Stage 3: trajectory generator (2026-10-18)

- `tasks/trajectory.py`: straight joint-space moves with timing laws `min_jerk` (quintic, the
  v0.3 profile) and `double_s` (time-optimal jerk-limited, 7 phases); `plan_segment` lengthens a
  move to the shortest duration that meets scaled velocity/acceleration/jerk limits and
  (optionally) a torque budget via inverse dynamics, or raises `TrajectoryInfeasible`
  (gravity alone over budget, waypoint outside the joint range). `check_reference` reports
  peak ratios and human-readable violations for any sampled reference.
- `JointTargetTask` / `CartesianReachTask` take the optional timing; without it the v0.3
  references are bit-identical (verified: same q/qd/qdd at sample times; benchmark tracking
  numbers unchanged). Datasheet acceleration/jerk limits added to `robots/panda.py`.
- With 0.9 v_max / 0.5 a_max / 0.05 jerk_max: `fast` moves become 1.585 / 1.341 / 1.0 s
  (min_jerk, joint 7 / joint 1 velocity-limited); `wrist` 2.223 / 3.193 / 2.0 s. double_s
  keeps all wrist moves at the requested 2.0 s (it cruises at the limit instead of peaking).

## Stage 4: constraint handling (2026-10-18)

- New metrics: predicted max |qd|/v_max and position excess of every accepted plan, max
  slack, actual max excess (rad/s), longest violation run, violation integral, and the
  reference's own velocity/acceleration/torque ratios (`reference_feasible`).
- Torque MPC, v0.3 settings: plan knots 1.0000 x v_max, actual 1.0002 at knots and 1.0006
  between knots (fast); 369 steps, longest 344 ms, integral 0.046 mrad. Violations are
  prediction error (1-step qd error <= 1.4e-3 rad/s) plus intersample motion, while the
  constraint is active at its bound.
- Tightening |qd| <= (1 - m) v_max: m = 0.5% -> actual 0.9955x (fast), 0.9957x (wrist), zero
  violating steps; lag +3% (169 -> 175 mrad, 532 -> 542). m = 1%: 0.990x, lag +7%. Chosen 0.5%
  (7x the measured prediction + intersample excess of 0.07%). No effect on feasible scenarios.
- Servo MPC: violations are intersample (knots 0.936x, between knots 1.06x on fast): the stiff
  servo answers each setpoint step with a velocity transient inside the interval. Knot
  tightening does not help (5%: unchanged; 10%: 1.47x, worse). Not adopted; documented.
  On wrist the servo MPC's 1.44x comes from its PID fallback (43 updates), which follows the
  infeasible reference at full speed (QP slack 1.1 rad/s after fallback).

## Stage 5: disturbance rejection (2026-10-18)

- `controllers/disturbance.py`: lumped joint-torque disturbance observer from measured motion
  and *applied* torque, using MuJoCo's discrete-time inverse dynamics (mjENBL_INVDISCRETE) on a
  private copy of the controller's model. CTC: tau = ID(...) - d_hat. Torque MPC: offset-free
  (d_hat as known input over the horizon) + optional 500 Hz feedthrough of d_hat changes.
  CTC also got integral action (ki < Kd Kp, conditional integration + clamp anti-windup).
- Exploration (push 30 N +y at the hand, 1 s; demo with masses -15%), max error mrad:
  CTC 35.2 / 4.98; CTC+I (ki 3000) 26.8 / 3.85; CTC+DOB T=20 ms 5.74 / 0.83; T=5 ms 1.61 / 0.23.
  Torque MPC 12.1 / 1.91; +DOB 20 ms 7.57 / 1.07; +DOB 20 ms feedthrough 5.33 / 0.77;
  +DOB 5 ms feedthrough 2.17 / 0.32. Steady deflection at the end of the push: CTC 35 mrad,
  MPC 11.7 -> all DOB variants <= 0.005 mrad. Nominal demo unchanged (d_hat ~ 0, exact model).
- Noise trade-off (new `sensor_noise` config: q 1e-4 rad, qd 1e-3 rad/s std): DOB 5 ms raises
  step-to-step torque jitter to 0.78 Nm (CTC, vs 0.21 without DOB) / 0.63 Nm (MPC, vs 0.36);
  10 ms: 0.45 / 0.36 Nm with push max 3.1 / 3.75 mrad; 20 ms: 0.31 / 0.32 Nm, 5.7 / 5.5 mrad.
  Chosen T = 10 ms with feedthrough for the MPC. Noise itself costs the torque MPC more than
  CTC on the demo (0.29 vs 0.09 mrad max): Q = 1e5 feeds position noise straight through.
- Robustness benchmark (`configs/benchmark_robustness.yaml`, results/v0.4_robustness):
  5 torque controllers x 18 scenarios, all finite, no fallbacks. Observer variants cut max
  error 4-10x for every mass error (+-5..25%) and every push direction; CTC+observer is
  slightly better than the v0.4 torque MPC on disturbances (e.g. push +y 3.14 vs 3.62 mrad,
  mass -25% 0.84 vs 0.95 mrad). CTC+integral helps mismatch RMS (3.07 -> 0.38 mrad at -25%)
  but only cuts push peaks 25% and settles slower (0.51 s vs 0.19 s).
