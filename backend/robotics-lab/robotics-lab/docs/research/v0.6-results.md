# v0.6 results: known-world physical pick-and-place and a payload study

Branch `feature/v0.6-known-world-manipulation` (from v0.5.2, `5dbeffd`). MuJoCo 3.16.0, Python 3.13.7,
NumPy 2.5.4, SciPy 1.18.1, OSQP 1.1.3, Windows 11. Progress log with every decision and failure:
`docs/research/v0.6-progress.md`.

## Summary

- **Milestone 1 (foundation): passed.** At the baseline 189 tests passed and the wall demo was unchanged.
  The torque MPC's assembled QP matches its documented objective and constraints on independent checks,
  including a hand-solved case. No correctness bug was found (section 1).
- **Milestone 2 (physical pick-and-place): passed.** The Panda grasps a free box top-down, lifts it, carries
  it (around a static obstacle in half the cases) and places it. All of this happens through simulated
  contact and friction, under an event-based supervisor that checks measured conditions. Development
  cases: 5/5 with CTC + observer and 5/5 with torque MPC + observer. Four negative controls were each
  rejected for their intended reason (section 2).
- **Milestone 3 (payload study): target met.** The primary configuration (torque MPC + observer) completed
  **30/30** frozen evaluation episodes with **0 forbidden contacts** (target: at least 27/30). The other
  three configurations also completed 30/30, so complete-task success does not separate the controllers
  in this suite. Tracking accuracy and clearance margins do (section 3).
- **What the evidence supports.** In these implementations the disturbance observer is what makes the
  arm insensitive to an unmodelled payload:
  - Without it, the end-effector error while carrying grows with mass: CTC median 0.61 → 4.77 mm and
    torque MPC 0.36 → 2.78 mm, from 0.15 to 1.15 kg.
  - The measured clearance of the robot and the carried object falls below the planned 20 mm, to
    14.8 mm (CTC) and 17.1 mm (MPC) at 1.15 kg.
  - With the observer, both controllers stay at or above 20.0 mm. Placement error stays ≤ 1.43 mm at
    every mass (0.67 mm median at 1.15 kg), against ≤ 3.37 mm without the observer.
  - Torque MPC + observer tracks most tightly while carrying (0.02–0.07 mm median vs 0.15–0.16 mm for
    CTC + observer). It costs about 2× the wall time per episode in this Python implementation (5.7 vs 2.9 s), and
    12 of 17,050 MPC updates overran their 20 ms budget.
  - CTC + observer is competitive on everything the task requires.
- **Main caveat.** Success with the 1.15 kg block depends on the contact-solver setting chosen during
  development (elliptic friction cone, impratio 10). With MuJoCo's default pyramidal cone the block creeps
  10 mm through the grasp and 0 of 6 sensitivity episodes succeed (section 5).

## 1. Foundation check (milestone 1)

Full suite at the baseline: 189 passed. The corrected wall demo still plans both targets and reaches them
(0.02 / 0.05 mm, 0 contact steps, 450 MPC updates, 0 fallbacks).

The torque MPC's QP had never been audited against its documented formulation (only the servo MPC's
had). `tools/audit_torque_mpc.py` checks it with code written independently of the controller's matrix
assembly (output `docs/research/v0.6-mpc-audit.json`, tests `tests/test_mpc_audit.py`):

| Check | Method | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Objective | documented cost evaluated term by term (tracking, terminal x5, effort above the gravity+d̂ hold torque normalized by τ_max, rate against the previously *applied* torque, quadratic + linear slack) vs ½zᵀPz + qᵀz, differences over 20 random z pairs (the constant cancels) | 2.5e-13 relative (875 variables, 1,575 rows) |
| Hand-checkable case | 1 joint, N = 2, known affine model; states eliminated by hand, 2×2 normal equations | first torque equal to 1e-12 Nm |
| Active torque limit | same case, limit 3.2 Nm below the unconstrained 3.28 Nm; bounded nested search | equal to 7e-8 Nm, plan at the limit |
| Known disturbance d̂ = 1 Nm | same case: prediction uses B(τ + d̂), hold torque τ_g − d̂ | equal to 1e-12; predicted states = hand recursion to 1e-16 |
| Constraint rows | A z for a hand-built z | dynamics residual 2.6e-15; torque rows = τ/τ_scale; state rows x+s, x−s, s; q bounds on q entries, velocity bounds (with the 0.5% margin) on q̇ entries; torque bounds = actuator limits |
| Feedthrough sign | d̂ rises by 1.5 Nm between updates | held torque drops by exactly 1.5 Nm |
| Observer sign on the plant | constant unmeasured joint torque via `qfrc_applied` | estimated with the same sign to 3e-13 Nm |
| Prediction vs nonlinear rollout | accepted plan replayed through MuJoCo, mid-motion (3 times) | 1 interval: 2e-9 to 2e-8 rad; 0.5 s horizon: 1.6 to 5.3 mrad; re-linearized at every step: ≤ 2e-8 rad |
| Statuses (wall demo, 9 s) | OSQP info per update | 450/450 solved, primal residual ≤ 2.7e-9, dual ≤ 7.5e-5, ≤ 175 iterations |

Conclusions. No implementation bug was found, so no controller behaviour changed (MPC updates now also
record OSQP's residuals). The horizon prediction error is the documented frozen-Jacobian approximation
(one linear model used for all 25 stages): replaying the same plan through a model re-linearized at each
step removes it. "Solved inaccurate" is accepted and counted (`inaccurate`); every other status or a
non-finite solution hands the joint torques to CTC until the next successful update.

## 2. What was built (milestone 2)

```
pick_place.py / pick_place_study.py
  -> PickPlaceEpisode (simulation/manipulation.py): supervisor, contact monitor, verifier
       phases: approach -> descend -> close -> lift -> hold -> transport -> lower -> release -> retreat -> verify
       |-- motion planning (planning/): pose-IK candidates + RRT-Connect, phase-dependent collision rules,
       |     carried object attached in the planner's private model only
       |-- references (tasks/pick_place.py): planner paths (v0.4/v0.5 timing), straight Cartesian lines
       |-- arm controller (unchanged classes): CTC or torque MPC, each with or without the observer
       |-- gripper: engine.set_gripper (actuator 8), never through the arm controller
       `-- plant: MuJoCo, free box moved only by contact and friction
```

- **Pose IK** (`ArmKinematics.solve_pose_ik`): error `[p* − p; w·log(R* Rᵀ)]` in the world frame with
  MuJoCo's world-frame rotational Jacobian; damped least squares with null-space bias. The rotational
  Jacobian matches finite differences to 6e-10. Random reachable poses converge; unreachable positions and
  an unreachable orientation (gripper pointing up at table height) are rejected. `solve_ik` is unchanged.
- **Straight approach/lift/lower/retreat lines** (`CartesianSegment`): pose IK every 5 mm along the line,
  seeded with the previous solution, joint jumps above 0.1 rad rejected; a cubic spline through the samples
  timed by the quintic, lengthened to the scaled velocity/acceleration limits. The *timed reference*
  (not just the IK samples) is checked by forward kinematics at 101 points: in the development runs every
  line stayed within 0.05 mm of the straight line and 0.07° of the commanded orientation (limits used by
  the supervisor: 1 mm, 0.5°).
- **Physical object and gripper**: `environments/pick_place.xml` (+ `pick_place_obstacle.xml`): a free
  50 × 50 × 60 mm box on the table; a static 160 × 40 × 180 mm box between pick and place regions in the
  obstacle scene. The object is never written, welded or attached in the plant. The Menagerie gripper
  servo squeezes a 50 mm box with only 2.5 N (enough for ~0.25 kg); manipulation configs set
  `robot.gripper_stiffness: 1500` N/m via MjSpec (~37 N squeeze; the real Franka Hand applies 70 N
  continuously). Same actuator, ctrl range and force range; the vendored XML is unchanged.
- **Contact solver of the plant**: `cone: elliptic, impratio: 10`. With MuJoCo's default pyramidal cone
  the soft contacts let the 0.4 kg box creep ~5 mm through the 37 N grip at friction 1 (a 1.0 kg box
  crept > 6 mm and was rejected). Physically a rigid grip at 4x the required friction does not creep, so
  this is a solver artifact; MuJoCo's documentation recommends the elliptic cone and impratio > 1 for
  grasping. The sensitivity check (section 5) shows what depends on it.
- **Supervisor**: each phase ends on measured conditions: arm at rest (|q̇| < 0.02 rad/s for 50 ms after
  the reference ends), fingers stopped (width change < 2 µm per step for 50 ms), measured object height,
  measured object pose in the hand frame, object speed. Every wait has a timeout and a reason. Poses are
  refreshed with `mj_kinematics` after every step, so decisions use the current state. The lowering line
  uses the grasp offset measured at the start of lowering (the object may slip during transport).
- **Collision rules** (planner side, `_configure_checker`): every pair keeps the 20 mm clearance except:
  hand/fingers vs object ≥ 5 mm while descending around it and retreating (fingers open: 15 mm per side
  by construction); finger–object contact allowed while the object is held; the object attached to the
  hand in the private model (its pose follows the hand; object–environment pairs need 20 mm; box–box
  pairs via `mj_geomDistance`; lever arms grow by its extent so the edge-sampling bound covers it);
  object–table ≥ −2 mm only on the lift-off and set-down lines. Planned transport paths are therefore
  checked with the *carried* object's geometry and the closed fingers.
- **Contact monitor** (plant side, every physics step, the contacts MuJoCo applied): forbidden are
  robot–environment, robot self-contact, hand/link–object, finger–object outside the grasp phases,
  object–floor/obstacle always, and object–table during hold and transport.
- **Controller model boundary**: `engine.contact_free_model()`: contacts disabled and the object's mass
  fixed at a nominal 0.4 kg. The controllers copy the full live state into their private data; with
  contacts on, `mj_inverse`/`mj_forward` would compute the finger–object contact forces from it and
  "see" the payload. Tested: inverse dynamics, gravity torque, and the MPC's affine prediction term are
  identical (1e-9) for object masses 0.15 and 1.15 kg and different object contact states at the same arm
  state; a control test shows the default model (contacts on) does change with the contact state.
- **Success criteria** (declared in `configs/pick_place.yaml` before the evaluation): lift ≥ 50 mm above
  the initial height; held ≥ 1.0 s with ≤ 5 mm displacement in the hand frame; no drop (≤ 10 mm
  displacement in the hand frame during transport and lowering); zero forbidden contacts at any step; after
  release and settling (speed < 5 mm/s and < 0.05 rad/s for 0.2 s), object centre within 10 mm (3-D) of the
  target and tilt ≤ 10°. Closing must leave the fingers within 4 mm of the object width.

Development (`results/v0.6/dev_ctc.json`, `dev_mpc.json`; five cases, three on the open table and two around
the obstacle, 0.4 kg): 5/5 with CTC + observer and 5/5 with torque MPC + observer (0 fallback steps, every
QP "solved"), 0 forbidden contacts, placement errors 0.1–2.4 mm.

Negative controls (`dev_free_1`, each must be rejected for its own reason; tests
`test_negative_controls_are_rejected_for_the_intended_reason`):

| Fault | Rejected at | Measured reason |
|---|---|---|
| gripper never closes | close | fingers at 80.0 mm, object 50 mm |
| Menagerie gripper gains (2.5 N), 1.0 kg | lift | object rose −0.1 mm (< 50 mm); the width check had passed |
| placement target belief 30 mm off | verify | object 29.9 mm from the target |
| gripper opens half-way through transport | transport | object displaced 10.6 mm in the hand (drop) |

A "low friction" control was tried and discarded: MuJoCo combines the friction of two geoms with the
maximum, so lowering only the object's friction left the pads' 1.0 in charge and the episode succeeded.

## 3. Payload study (milestone 3)

**Question.** How does disturbance estimation affect physical manipulation with an unmodelled payload, and
what trade-off does torque MPC offer compared with computed-torque control? This compares the four
*implemented configurations* below. It is not a general claim about the algorithms.

**Design (frozen in `configs/pick_place_study.yaml` before any evaluation run, commit `d723e8a`).**

- **Configurations, at their actual update rates:**
  - CTC without observer (500 Hz);
  - CTC + observer (500 Hz, T = 10 ms);
  - torque MPC without observer (QP at 50 Hz, torque held 20 ms; CTC fallback);
  - torque MPC + observer (QP at 50 Hz plus the observer's 500 Hz feedthrough).
  All other settings are the defaults of `configs/default.yaml`.
- **Masses:** 0.15, 0.40 and 1.15 kg, i.e. a plastic, an aluminium and a steel block of 50 × 50 × 60 mm.
  These were chosen after checking the limits. The grip is about 37 N at friction 1, giving roughly
  3.7 kg static capacity. At the wrist the payload torque is about 1.5 Nm against the 12 Nm limit.
- **Cases:** 10 evaluation cases (object pose, target, planning seed) generated once with a fixed RNG
  seed and disjoint from the development cases. Cases 0–4 are on the open table and 5–9 are around the
  obstacle.
- **Same inputs for every configuration:** the same cases, initial state, grasp policy, planner seeds and
  reference construction. The references are built from the measured state, so they are matched but not
  bit-identical when the grasp differs.
- **Fixed controller model:** the controllers' nominal dynamics are the same for every mass (contact-free
  model, nominal 0.4 kg object that cannot act on the arm; section 2). Only the plant's object mass
  changes.
- **Denominator:** every attempt counts. 120 episodes were run sequentially and none was skipped or
  removed.
- **Measurement:** contacts, torque saturation and joint position/velocity limits are checked at every
  physics step (2 ms). Clearance is measured every 10 ms with `mj_geomDistance` on the plant.

**Results** (`docs/research/v0.6-study-summary.csv`, per episode `v0.6-study-episodes.jsonl`). "Carrying"
covers the hold, transport and lower phases. Clearance is the minimum over the 10 episodes; for the
object it covers hold and transport.

Across all 120 episodes:

- **Limits:** 0 physics steps had torque saturation, joint-position-limit violations or speed-limit
  violations.
- **Joint error:** the largest joint tracking error was 9.9 mrad for CTC without observer, 3.6 mrad for
  MPC without observer, 0.54 mrad for CTC + observer and 0.86 mrad for MPC + observer.
- **Duration:** about 11.4 s of simulated time per episode.
- **Grasp:** slip in the hand stayed ≤ 2.3 mm during carrying and ≤ 0.2 mm while holding. Placement
  errors of about 0.7 mm with the observer come mainly from this slip, not from tracking.

MPC operation (60 MPC episodes; `mpc` block of each episode record):

| | MPC, no observer | MPC + observer |
|---|---|---|
| QP updates | 17,050 | 17,050 |
| status | 17,050 "solved" | 17,050 "solved" |
| fallback updates / physics steps under CTC fallback | 0 / 0 | 0 / 0 |
| solve time, mean of episode means / worst episode p95 | 4.11 / 8.50 ms | 3.86 / 8.00 ms |
| worst update (linearize + build + solve) | 55.0 ms | 26.0 ms |
| updates over the 20 ms budget | 16 | 12 |
| max primal / dual residual | 1.7e-8 / 1.9e-4 | 2.5e-8 / 1.9e-4 |

Every MPC episode was executed by the MPC: no physics step was under the fallback. The budget overruns are
wall-clock only, because the simulation waits for the controller. On a real-time system they would be
missed deadlines (Python on Windows, not a real-time implementation).

**Interpretation.**

- **Observer vs no observer** (the clearest effect):
  - Without the observer, both controllers deflect in proportion to the payload: the carrying error
    grows almost linearly with mass. The MPC deflects less than CTC (2.78 vs 4.77 mm at 1.15 kg) because
    its position weight makes it stiffer than CTC's ωn = 25 rad/s; neither removes the offset.
  - The sag erodes the planner's clearance. The 15 mm certificate assumes exact tracking, and with
    CTC without observer at 1.15 kg the carried block came within **14.8 mm** of the environment, just
    under that bound. Clearance is a property of planner plus controller, not of the planner alone.
  - With the observer, the payload is estimated from joint motion within about 10 ms. Tracking,
    placement and clearance are then independent of mass within measurement scatter.
- **MPC vs CTC with the observer:**
  - The MPC tracks 2–8× more tightly while carrying (median) and also enforces speed limits predictively (not
    exercised here: no speed-limit violation in any configuration).
  - It costs more computation and has timing overruns.
  - For this task (slow, feasible references, 10 mm placement tolerance) CTC + observer performs
    equally on every task-level outcome.
- **Complete-task success** is 30/30 for every configuration, so this suite cannot rank them on
  success. The payloads and tolerances are within the margins of all four.

## 4. Failures encountered (all retained in the progress log)

- **Development bugs, all fixed before the evaluation was frozen:**
  - Lowering used the grasp offset from the start of transport, so a slipped object was driven into
    the table (rejected by the line's collision check).
  - Release ended before the fingers were fully open, so the retreat check saw 4.8 mm < 5 mm.
  - The release-during-transport fault restarted the motion segment.
  - The "low friction" negative control was ineffective (max-combination rule).
- **Contact-solver dependence:** found in development (5 mm creep with the default cone) and confirmed by
  the sensitivity check (section 5).
- **Evaluation:** no failures in the 120 episodes.
- **Sensitivity check:** 8 failures, all slip/drop under alternative contact settings.

## 5. Sensitivity check (bounded)

Three evaluation cases (eval_02 open table, eval_06 and eval_09 around the obstacle), 1.15 kg (the hardest
mass), CTC + observer and torque MPC + observer, six variants of the plant's numerics; 36 episodes
(`docs/research/v0.6-sensitivity-summary.csv`, `v0.6-sensitivity-episodes.jsonl`).

| Variant | CTC + obs | MPC + obs | Max slip in the hand while carrying | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline (2 ms, elliptic cone, impratio 10) | 3/3 | 3/3 | 2.2 mm | placement ≤ 0.97 mm |
| timestep 1 ms | 3/3 | 3/3 | 2.3 mm | not a step-size artifact |
| all sliding friction × 0.6 | 3/3 | 3/3 | 2.2 mm | grip margin is large |
| noslip solver (5 iterations) | 3/3 | 3/3 | 1.4 mm (hold: 0.0) | rigid-friction post-processing: less creep than the baseline |
| elliptic cone, impratio 1 | 2/3 | 2/3 | 9.5 mm (failed: 10 mm) | eval_06 dropped while lowering |
| **pyramidal cone (MuJoCo default), impratio 1** | **0/3** | **0/3** | hold 2.8 mm, then ≥ 10 mm | all dropped in transport or lowering |

The grasp of the heavy block depends on how MuJoCo's soft contacts treat friction, not on the timestep or
on the friction coefficient. With the noslip solver, which removes creep by enforcing the friction cone
rigidly, slip is even smaller than in the baseline. This supports the view that the creep is a numerical
artifact of soft contacts and that the baseline setting is closer to rigid Coulomb friction. It also means
the heavy-mass results should not be quoted without the contact-solver setting. The controllers are not
implicated: CTC and MPC fail identically.

## 6. Limitations

- **Known world:** object pose, size and obstacles are exact ground truth read at the start of each phase.
  There is no perception, no pose uncertainty, and no replanning during a motion.
- **Grasp:** one top-down grasp policy, a fixed yaw, and box objects of one size. The gripper is stiffened
  from the Menagerie gains (documented model change). Friction is 1.0 (max of pad and box).
- **Contact numerics:** plant results depend on the contact solver (section 5). Only one simulator was
  used; no claim is made about real hardware.
- **Collision guarantees:**
  - The planner's certificate (≥ 15 mm between edge samples) assumes exact tracking. The study shows
    tracking error subtracts from it.
  - Cartesian lines are checked as polylines of their IK samples; the spline deviates from them by
    < 0.05 mm.
  - Self-collision remains discrete.
- **Statistics:** 10 cases × 3 masses per configuration. With 30/30 everywhere, the 95% upper bound on the
  per-episode failure rate is about 10% per configuration (rule of three). The tracking and clearance
  differences are large compared with their spread, but the cases are not a random sample of
  real-world tasks.
- **Timing:** wall-clock numbers are from one Windows laptop running Python, not a real-time system.
- **Provenance:** the main study ran on committed code (`d723e8a`). The sensitivity run had one
  uncommitted, behaviour-neutral change: the `on_step` hook in `PickPlaceEpisode.run`, committed
  afterwards. The `git_dirty_code: true` flag in `v0.6-study-provenance.json` is a false positive: the
  check matched untracked scripts in `docs/guide_src/`, and has since been fixed to ignore untracked
  files.

## 8. For a second-year mechanical engineering student

**What changed.** Until v0.5 the robot only moved its own arm. Now it handles a real (simulated) object.
The box has mass and friction, and the only thing that lifts it is the friction between the finger pads
and its sides, exactly as when you pinch a book. If the grip is too weak or the simulation's friction is
too "soft", the box slides out, and the program measures that and reports it.

**Why each layer exists.**

- **Supervisor (the "foreman"):** it decides what happens next from measurements, e.g. "the fingers
  stopped at 50 mm, so we are holding a 50 mm box; the box rose 100 mm, so it really was lifted". Without
  it the robot would replay a script and never notice a failed grasp.
- **Planner:** it finds a route around the obstacle with 20 mm of air everywhere. While carrying, it
  treats the box as part of the robot, because the box can hit things too.
- **Pose IK and straight lines:** the gripper must come down vertically without twisting. Otherwise it
  knocks the box over before it closes.
- **Controller:** it turns the planned motion into joint torques 500 times per second (CTC), or plans
  the torques 0.5 s ahead 50 times per second (MPC).
- **Disturbance observer:** the controllers' model of the arm does not include the box. When the box
  pulls the hand down, the observer notices that the arm accelerates differently from what the model
  predicts, works out the missing torque, and adds it back, much like integral action in a PID loop
  but faster.
- **Verifier and negative controls:** a test that never fails proves nothing, so we deliberately broke
  the grasp, the grip, the placement and the carry, and checked that each was caught for the right reason.

**What the evidence supports.**

- All four controller set-ups completed all 30 pick-and-place cycles.
- Without the observer, a 1.15 kg block sags the hand by up to 6 mm (CTC) or 3.4 mm (MPC). That sag eats
  into the safety gap the planner left around obstacles: 14.8 mm instead of 20 mm.
- With the observer the sag almost disappears (≤ 0.34 mm) for every mass.
- The MPC tracks more precisely than CTC but needs about twice the computing time. For this task, a
  well-tuned CTC with an observer is just as good.
- Finally, the result for the heavy block depends on a numerical setting of the simulator's friction
  model. A simulation is a model, and its numerical choices have to be tested like any other assumption.
