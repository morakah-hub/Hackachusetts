## Stage 1: MPC audit (2026-10-17)

Reproduction: `benchmark.py` on this branch reproduced every v0.2 tracking metric and fallback
count exactly (deterministic). Wall-clock timing did not reproduce: with other jobs running,
50 Hz overruns rose from 0-5 to 2-26 per run and 100 Hz demo went from 2 to 218. Solve times
in this session are ~2x the v0.2 session even when idle (machine/session variance).

Findings (tools in `tools/`):

1. Objective and constraints are mathematically correct: `tools/audit_mpc_objective.py`
   matches 1/2 z'Pz + q'z to a term-by-term evaluation to 6e-14 (relative), and dynamics and
   torque rows to 1e-14. cond(P) = 8.8e3; constraint entries span 2.9e-7 to 167 (tiny ones
   are finite-difference noise; zeroing them changes nothing).
2. Fallback cause (`tools/audit_fallback.py`): at the first wrist fallback no soft constraint
   is active, yet OSQP needs 1775 iterations. The sustained servo torque
   kp(u_k - q_{k+1}) - kd qd_{k+1} is almost independent of u_k (d/du = -0.002..0.027): it
   nearly cancels through the servo dynamics. The effort cost (weight up to 2.8e4) and the
   torque rows act on that cancelling combination, which ill-conditions the reduced problem.
   Effort 0: 800 iterations; torque rows off: 825; both off: 350. A condensed formulation of
   the same QP gives the same u0 (2.8e-9 rad) in 475 iterations but 94 ms (dense, 420 vars x
   1155 rows), so it is not a usable fix as is.
3. **Bug fixed:** after a fallback, the next update anchored the linearization point, the
   command-rate penalty and the slew limit to the stale last MPC command instead of the PID
   command the servo was actually holding. Fallbacks cascaded. Fix: track the command actually
   sent (`_applied`). Wrist: fallbacks 275 -> 43 of 450, overruns 236 -> 31. With MPC really in
   control, wrist tracking is 3.1 mrad RMS / 47.8 mrad max (v0.2's 1.7 / 24.9 were mostly PID).
   Other scenarios unchanged. Regression test:
   `test_recovery_after_fallback_anchors_to_applied_command` (fails on v0.2 code).
4. Rejected mitigations (measured): solver eps 1e-4 (wrist max error 494 mrad, fallbacks
   unchanged); effort weight 0.01/0 (demo slightly better, wrist fallbacks unchanged).
5. Horizon/rate sweep (before the fix): wrist fallbacks ~60% at every N in {5..30} and at
   50/100 Hz; solve time grows ~linearly with N; demo best at 100 Hz N=10 (6.2 mrad max).
6. State feedback: confirmed by existing tests (perturbed state -> different command).
7. Remaining structural issue: servo-setpoint MPC cannot express torque well (spikes,
   cancellation). Stage 2 (torque actuation) addresses this directly.

## Stage 2: torque actuation and torque MPC (2026-10-17)

- `Panda(actuation="torque")` turns actuators 1-7 into torque motors via MjSpec (gain 1, no bias,
  ctrlrange = forcerange = 87/12 Nm). Mass, inertia, armature, damping, joint limits and geometry
  are identical (tested); the vendored XML is untouched. Keyframe arm ctrl is zeroed (setpoints
  would otherwise become torques). Verified: actuator_force == qfrc_actuator == command.
- `controllers/dynamics.py` (`ArmInverseDynamics`): mj_inverse on scratch data. Torques reproduce
  commanded accelerations to 5e-4 rad/s^2. Tasks now also give `qdd` (min-jerk acceleration).
- `controllers/computed_torque.py`: CTC baseline (500 Hz, wn = 25 rad/s, zeta = 1). Fallback of the torque MPC.
- `controllers/torque_mpc.py`: linear (LTI-per-update) MPC on normalized torques v = tau/tau_max.
  Torque limits are hard and exact (no servo, no spike).
- Prediction accuracy with torque inputs is worse than with servos (no servo to stabilize the
  open loop): 16 mrad over 15 x 20 ms (10% of motion) vs 2 mrad over 15 x 10 ms.
- Numerics fixed during development (measured):
  * Raw-torque variables: P diagonal spanned ~10 orders of magnitude, OSQP polishing failed,
    at-rest plan drifted 2.7 mrad. Normalizing torques: polishing succeeds, drift 1e-9 rad,
    hold torque exact to 6.5e-7 Nm, constrained-scenario solve p95 ~15 -> ~6 ms.
  * slack_linear 1e5 too weak at Q = 1e5 (2.5 rad step planned 3.8x v_max). 1e6 is exact (slack
    6e-16) and cut iterations 1475 -> 450.
- Tuning (rate x horizon x Q): 100 Hz with N = 30 overran its 10 ms budget under constraints (~140
  overruns). Chose 50 Hz, N = 25 (0.5 s preview), Q = 1e5: demo 2.8 urad max, push 12 mrad,
  0 fallbacks, solve p95 <= 6 ms.
- Comparison (same scenarios, torque actuators): CTC demo 63 urad max, torque MPC 2.8 urad, same
  RMS torque (15.64 Nm). Push: CTC deflects 35 mrad (no integral action), torque MPC 12 mrad,
  servo PID 3.4 mrad (stiff servos). Over-speed references (fast, wrist): torque MPC holds joints
  at their speed limit (<= 1.02x) and accepts large tracking error (170 / 532 mrad); CTC follows
  the reference at 1.43x v_max.
- Caveat to test in Stage 4: all model-based controllers use the exact plant model.

## Stage 3: Cartesian reaching (2026-10-17)

- `simulation/kinematics.py` (`ArmKinematics`): FK, site Jacobian (matches finite differences to
  3e-7), position-only damped-least-squares IK with joint clamping (margin 0.05 rad) and a
  null-space pull to the home posture, collision checks at the goal and along the sampled joint
  path. Scratch data only.
- `tasks/reach.py` (`CartesianReachTask`): resolves targets through an injected planner, skips
  rejected targets with a reason, reuses min-jerk joint references.
- `configs/reach.yaml` (`extends: default.yaml`): 3 reachable targets, 1 out of reach
  (closest 361 mm), 1 inside the table (collision at goal). CLI: `--target X Y Z` (repeatable).
- All four controllers reach the 3 targets with end-effector error 0.00-0.08 mm at the end of each
  hold (dominated by the 0.1 mm IK tolerance); no contacts, no MPC fallbacks.
- Limitation demonstrated by a test: in `obstacles`, a target that is reachable from home is
  rejected from another start because the straight joint path hits an obstacle. Collision
  checking, not planning.

## Stage 5: performance (2026-10-17)

- Profiling (`tools/profile_mpc.py`; per-call timing without a profiler attached): per update,
  OSQP solve 1.6 ms (servo) / 3.1 ms (torque), OSQP matrix update (KKT refactorization) 0.75 /
  1.0 ms, linearization ~0.8 ms, Python QP assembly ~0.6 ms. cProfile misattributed most of the
  solve time to `OSQP.update`; the per-call timing above is the reliable one.
- Changes (measured with `tools/time_mpc.py 3`, median of 3 demo runs):
  * mjd_transitionFD forward instead of centered differences: 0.35 -> 0.18 ms; Jacobians change by
    <= 1e-6 (relative ~1e-7).
  * One plausibility check per update instead of two (eigenvalues computed once).
  * Servo MPC writes A/B into fixed CSC slots instead of re-concatenating the constraint vector.
  * Result: servo MPC 3.67 -> 3.35 ms (-9%), p95 4.36 -> 3.95 ms; torque MPC 5.15 -> 5.08 ms
    (within noise; dominated by OSQP). Tracking errors identical to 4 digits; QP audit unchanged.
- Rejected (no reliable gain): OSQP check_termination 5 (10-20% in easy cases, worse p95 in
  wrist), polishing off (torque push 12.1 -> 14.4 mrad).
- Physics timestep (2 ms) and control rate are independent (test for 50/100/250 Hz).
